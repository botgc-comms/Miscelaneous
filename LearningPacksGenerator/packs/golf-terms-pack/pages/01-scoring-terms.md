# Scoring Words

Understanding how to keep score in golf is an essential skill for junior golfers. Here are some important terms related to scoring that will help you navigate the game with confidence.

### Key Terms

- **Par**: The standard number of strokes that an expert golfer is expected to take to complete a hole or a course. Knowing the par helps you gauge your performance.

- **Birdie**: Scoring one stroke under par on a hole. For example, if a hole has a par of 4, making it in 3 strokes means you’ve achieved a birdie!

- **Eagle**: Scoring two strokes under par on a hole. If you manage to finish a par 5 hole in 3 strokes, you’ve made an eagle!

- **Bogey**: Scoring one stroke over par on a hole. If you take 5 strokes on a par 4 hole, that’s a bogey.

- **Double Bogey**: Scoring two strokes over par. For instance, if you need 6 strokes to complete a par 4 hole, that’s a double bogey.

- **Scorecard**: A card or sheet where you write down your scores during a round. It’s important to keep it neat and accurate!

### Tips for Keeping Score:

1. **Record Each Hole**: After finishing each hole, write down your score immediately. This helps avoid confusion later on.

2. **Know the Par of Each Hole**: Familiarise yourself with the par for each hole on the course. This will help you track your performance as you play.

3. **Practice Regularly**: The more you practice, the better you’ll understand how scoring works! Play with friends or family to enhance your skills.

4. **Ask Questions**: If you’re unsure about a term or how to score, don’t hesitate to ask your coach or more experienced players.

As you continue your golfing journey, using these scoring words will not only improve your game but also help you communicate better with your fellow golfers. Enjoy the challenge and the fun that comes with learning to score!
