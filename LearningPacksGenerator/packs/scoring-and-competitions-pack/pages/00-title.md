# Scoring Basics

Understanding how to keep score is a fundamental part of enjoying golf and enhancing your skills. Let’s break it down into simple steps, so both you and your parents can grasp the essentials.

### Stroke Play

In stroke play, the aim is to complete the course in the fewest number of strokes possible. Each time you hit the ball, that counts as one stroke. At the end of the round, you tally up the total strokes taken. Lower scores win!

**Key Terms:**
- **Par**: The expected number of strokes for a hole. For example, if a hole is a par 4, it means a good player should aim to finish that hole in 4 strokes.
- **Birdie**: Completing the hole in one stroke under par (e.g., 3 strokes on a par 4).
- **Bogey**: Completing the hole in one stroke over par (e.g., 5 strokes on a par 4).

### Match Play

In match play, two players (or teams) compete against each other hole by hole. The player with the fewer strokes on a hole wins that hole. The overall winner of the match is the one who wins the most holes.

**Understanding Wins:**  
If Player A wins 2 holes and Player B wins 1, Player A is ahead 2 up. If Player A is 2 up with 2 holes to play, they’ve already secured their match because Player B can’t catch up.

### Keeping Score

**Using a Scorecard:**  
A scorecard is a simple way to keep track of your scores during a round. Fill in your name, the date, and the course you’re playing. Record your scores for each hole and add them up at the end. This is also a great way to see improvements over time!

**Parent Support:**  
Parents can help by asking about your round after you’ve finished. Discuss your scores, which holes were challenging, and what you enjoyed the most. This reflection can boost your interest and keep you motivated.
