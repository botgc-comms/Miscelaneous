# Lightning and Warning Horns

Golf is an outdoor sport, and while it can be enjoyable, it’s important to stay safe, especially when the weather is unpredictable. One of the key safety concerns on the course is lightning. Here’s what you need to know:

## Recognising the Signs

1. **Darkening Skies**: If the clouds start to look menacing or dark, it’s time to be cautious.
2. **Sudden Changes in Temperature**: A sudden drop in temperature can also indicate an approaching storm.

## What to Do When Thunder Strikes

- **Listen for Warning Horns**: Many golf courses have a warning system in place. If you hear three distinct blasts of a horn, it means you should immediately seek shelter. This can be in a clubhouse, car, or another safe building.
- **Take Shelter**: Do not wait to finish your game. Your safety is more important than completing a hole.

## After the Storm

- **Wait for the All-Clear**: Once the storm has passed, you may hear a single long blast of the horn. This signals that it is safe to return to the course.
- **Check the Weather**: Before heading back out, make sure to check the weather. Continuing to play in adverse conditions can lead to further dangers.

## Staying Safe 

1. **Don’t Stand Under Trees**: Tall trees can attract lightning. If you find yourself caught outside, look for a building or low ground to wait it out.
2. **Avoid Metal Objects**: Stay away from fences, golf clubs, and other metal items during a storm, as these can conduct electricity.

By understanding these signals and knowing what steps to take, you’ll be well-prepared to ensure your safety on the golf course, even when the weather takes a turn.
