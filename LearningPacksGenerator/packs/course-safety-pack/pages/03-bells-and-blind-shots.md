# Bells and Blind Shots

As you continue your golfing journey, understanding course etiquette and safety is crucial. One key aspect to be aware of is the use of bells and the significance of blind shots.

### Bells

Many golf courses have bell systems at certain tee boxes, especially where visibility is limited. A bell is there to alert other players that someone is about to tee off. 

- **When to Use**: If you're on a tee that overlooks a fairway or a green and you can't see whether it's clear, ring the bell before you hit your shot. 

- **How to Use**: Approach the bell and give it a good ring. After that, wait a moment to ensure it's safe before striking your ball.

### Blind Shots

A blind shot occurs when you can't see where your ball is going because of a hill, tree, or other obstructions.

- **Assess the Area**: Look around carefully from the tee box. If you can’t see the landing area, think about how to safely proceed.

- **Communicate**: If you're unsure whether it's safe to play, consider asking a playing partner or waiting for those ahead before taking your shot.

### Important Reminders

- **Always Check**: Before you take any shot, especially on blind holes, always check for other players in the area.
  
- **Be Patient**: It’s not just about making your shot; patience ensures everyone’s safety and enhances the enjoyment of the round.

### Practise Safety Habits

Integrate these safety habits into your game so they become second nature. As you grow in skill and confidence, you'll not only play better but also ensure a safe experience for everyone on the course.
